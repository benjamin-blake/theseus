# Executor Persona Substrate and Billing Shape Audit

## Q6 - insufficient-evidence

Do not ratify a layer-2 substrate yet. The evidence gathered does not discriminate to one substrate, and the remaining blockers are few, cheap, and already scoped. With persona inference routing settled by Decision 122 (LiteLLM tiers for the executor; claude -p confined to scheduled agents), the incumbent - AWS Lambda durable functions, CD.27 as designed - leads as contracted; long_running_container is its genuine head-to-head rival under a rescoped, restart-tolerant contract; and the choice between them turns on decisions and measurements the repo has not yet made. Deferring is not timidity here: naming a winner before the workspace decision and the spike would be commitment before evidence, the exact failure mode Decision 75 records. One fairness note, accepted from adversarial review: the incumbent's lead is measured against contract text this audit itself rates defective (ESB-05, ESB-08), and under this prompt's rules an insufficiency verdict leaves the incumbent rated at Q3/Q5 - a structural soft default that must not be read as ratification.

## Decisive evidence

Four facts carry most of the weight. First, the durable-functions semantics are real and platform-verified: waits and callbacks suspend without compute charges, checkpointed operations are skipped on replay, executions run to one year - but the SDK checkpoints step return values, not the filesystem, so the git workspace the two heavy personas mutate sits entirely outside the checkpoint model, and no tier item owns its backing store or resume semantics (ESB-01, the audit's highest-severity finding and the largest open discriminator). Second, Decision 122 already decided persona routing - a correction adversarial review forced on this audit's own draft - leaving only a rationale-coherence gap (ESB-03). Third, CD.27's maturity hedge points at a control that does not exist: the fallback's promised "INTENT Open Question re-eval at each T4.2 atomic-plan filing" has no carrier anywhere in the repo, and the prior executor audit already dismissed a maturity-risk challenge by citing that phantom control (ESB-02, high). Fourth, economics cannot decide this question: every candidate's substrate adder lands in single dollars per month at the recorded scale of 100 recs/month against ~$5/month of inference.

## Direct answers Q1-Q5

**Q1 - durable-provides-materially-more**, as contracted. Long-run execution, checkpointing, completed-operation suppression, local testing, and execution history arrive platform-side; alternatives hand-roll them at S-M each. The verdict is deliberately narrow: suppression is at-least-once (a crash between an LLM response and its checkpoint re-issues the call under every substrate), replay determinism is a per-persona discipline with no enforcing check (ESB-06), and the conversation transcript - which durable rebuilds free on replay - is the one clear differential against the SFN-per-iteration alternative. Under a substrate-neutral restatement of the contract, the differential collapses to managedness plus cents.

**Q2 - partially-movable.** An LLM call inside a durable step is billed compute for its full wall-clock; only waits and callbacks suspend billing. DeepSeek (Tier 1) is synchronous-only: immovable. Anthropic's Message Batches (async, ~1h, 50% off) is reachable only via a LiteLLM proxy passthrough deployment or a CD.28 amendment. SFN's http:invoke could hold a sync call unbilled but bypasses the LiteLLM discipline and its ~60s-class timeout likely forecloses reasoner calls (unverified).

**Q2a - routing-constrains-substrate.** A CLI routing demands a CLI-hosting runtime and would collapse the viable set to container/runner classes; an HTTP routing is runtime-agnostic. Decision 122 resolved routing to LiteLLM, so the constraint operates prospectively - the reopen consequence should be recorded (ESB-03).

**Q3 - conformant-only-with-changes** (rating the incumbent). In-flight loop state lives inside the compute layer, pinned to function version and SDK major - the weakest compute-interchangeability profile in the set. Conformance requires: an executable fallback spec with a real re-evaluation carrier (ESB-02), an SDK pin/alias delivery-path rule, and the workspace backing-store decision (ESB-01). eu-west-2 availability needs a re-probe (current docs publish no region matrix).

**Q4 - load-bearing.** "Regular Lambdas are deterministic-only" protects iterative loops from losing in-flight state at the 15-minute ceiling and from deterministic retry re-issuing completed LLM calls. But it binds only for the heavy personas, is contingent on the Lambda-class assignment itself, and - read literally - outlaws Decision 39's LLM-backed `agent` state type. One scoping sentence reconciles them (ESB-10).

**Q5 - reversible-with-material-cost** (rating the incumbent). Contracts survive any swap (persona I/O, S3 artefacts, layer-1 state machine, T4.9a callback, budget counters, telemetry); persona internals do not. Exit prices at XS contract cost throughout and S-to-M implementation; concurrency=1 makes draining a one-execution kill-and-refile. The disclosed asymmetry: the incumbent's modal exit trigger (a durable API regression) is disorderly and calendar-pressured; no self-owned-loop alternative carries that tail.

## Billing shape, in one paragraph

At the project's own recorded scale, the substrate choice is economically free. Model-latency wall-clock is billed compute under every AWS candidate (roughly $1-8/month gross), a Fargate container costs about $5-15/month, and GitHub-hosted runners cost $0 in dollars on this public repo - all against ~$5/month of inference. Nothing here should influence the substrate decision. The only terms that could ever matter: a NAT gateway (~$32/month managed, ~$3-5 as an instance) if an EFS-backed Lambda workspace is chosen; a repo-going-private event, which reprices the runner class to order $50-150/month; and the concurrency raise T4.1 itself schedules, which reopens all of this. The repo's cost record needs repair regardless - its one SFN line overstates its own arithmetic 13-44x (ESB-09).

## Adversarial-review effect

Three rounds, twelve fresh-context reviewers, none stable under the strict trigger rule - each round established at least one factual error in the draft, which is what the process is for. Review reversed no question verdict but rewrote the findings register: it corrected the draft's two largest claims (routing was decided, not open; a comparative evaluation exists, incompletely), discovered the phantom fallback carrier and its prior-audit false dismissal, created two findings the auditor missed entirely (the executor-infrastructure deploy-path gap; the replay-rate gate incoherence), killed an EFS factual error, rebuilt the billing envelope, and forced the Q6 disclosure that insufficiency structurally defaults to the incumbent. Final-round pressure split three ways (incumbent, container, set-incomplete) - consistent with, and corroborating, the insufficient-evidence verdict. Every challenge and reconciliation is recorded in the YAML.
